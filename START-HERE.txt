TRANSITSENSE — 3D SMART CITY SIMULATION

OPEN THE WEBSITE
Download transitsense.html from this folder and open it in a current Chrome, Edge,
Firefox, or Safari browser. Google Drive previews do not execute websites: download
first. Everything needed for the 3D simulation is included in the HTML file.
Google Fonts is optional; offline, the site uses system fonts.

THE CITY
Both intersections exist together: a quiet residential crossing and a busy urban
junction. The residential lanes are 4.8 m wide with four short red/green warning
posts and varied detached homes. The busy junction retains four overhead signals.
Use City overview, Residential lanes, or Central junction to change the camera.
Traffic queues with vehicle-size spacing. The busy junction obeys signals; the
residential junction yields in arrival order without timed traffic signals.

THE FOUR DEMONSTRATIONS
Blind-junction warning: send an approaching car to keep its approach post green and turn the other
three posts red. All four return to green after the crossing clears. Compare with a departing or parked car. Test the sensor fault to see a
steady red fail-safe state.
Ambulance priority: dispatch an ambulance into the busy four-way intersection.
Signals first clear traffic, then hold its approach green until it passes.
The siren plays through your speakers (use the speaker button to mute).
A reflected-siren test plays a faint echo and demonstrates rejection without changing the signal cycle.
Magnetic signature: two-wheelers, cars, and trucks pass embedded sensors.
Counts and example magnetic waveforms update at each detected pass. Choose a
vehicle and send it through the sensor.
Predictive parking: a parking building with three zones and one camera per zone.
Choose your arrival time: the panel predicts whether a bay will be free when you
arrive and which one (with a backup), and highlights it. Simulate my arrival replays
the expected departures and arrivals, then drives your car to the predicted bay.
Rush hour makes the garage fill up, so you can see the "no bay" case and the
suggested later arrival time. Show or hide the roof.

REAL HARDWARE
Click any sensor label marked "i" in the city, or "Hardware & code" in the panel,
to see the real sensor (3D model), how it works, parts, ESP32 wiring and the
Arduino code. The same sketches are in the hardware folder.

CONTROLS
Drag to orbit. Scroll or pinch to zoom. Right-drag to pan.
Use the + / - buttons to zoom, 0 to reset the camera, and Space to pause.
The playback control offers 0.5x, 1x, and 2x speeds.
Reduced-motion preferences start the simulation paused.

SOURCE PROJECT
The dist directory contains the readable HTML, CSS, JavaScript, pure simulation
rules, and bundled Three.js library and license; check.mjs and traffic-check.mjs are
small verification checks. To edit: open files in an editor, and serve the dist
directory with any static web server. For Python: python3 -m http.server 4173 --directory dist
Then open http://localhost:4173. After editing, run node build.mjs to rebuild
transitsense.html. To run checks: node check.mjs and node traffic-check.mjs
No package installation, account or API keys are required.

SCOPE
This is an interactive concept showcase. Vehicles follow simplified straight-through
routes. In the website, sensor readings are scripted illustrations rather than real
signal processing, and no microphone or camera is used. Parking uses synthetic
arrival and departure rates; the bay prediction illustrates the method and is not a
measured accuracy claim. The ESP32 sketches in the hardware folder compile but have
not been tested on physical hardware.
Not validated for real-road or emergency-control use.

Built for the requested four-feature showcase, with no landing page or dashboard.
Not published to Sites. Sources inspired by the user-provided CityVerse and Road Mesh
references, with original city geometry and interface.
